Description

Entry 092 is a multi-story stairwell of unknown dimensions. The structure presents as a standard institutional emergency staircase, painted institutional gray with numbered floor markers at regular intervals.

The stairwell exhibits recursive architectural properties. Floor numbering continues indefinitely in the ascending direction. No structural endpoint has been documented. No basement floor has been successfully reached despite multiple downward expeditions.

Crucially: ascending the stairwell does not reverse the descent. Researchers report that climbing upward from any floor consistently results in reaching higher numbered floors, yet the stairwell does not appear to terminate. After reaching Floor 47, architectural characteristics change markedly. Beyond Floor 52, physical properties become increasingly anomalous.

Most concerning: the stairwell demonstrates awareness of occupant intent. Descent attempts frequently result in personnel finding themselves ascending despite deliberate downward movement. The structure appears to redirect navigation toward higher floors.

Structural Documentation

  • Entry Point: Floor 0 (designated "Ground Level" on signage, though subterranean location)
  • Standard Floors (0-12): Institutional gray walls, numbered markers, standard staircase dimensions (3.5 feet wide, 7-foot ceiling)
  • Intermediate Floors (13-46): Wall texture becomes smoother; numbering becomes inconsistent (occasional floors skipped or repeated)
  • Anomalous Floors (47-52+): Stair treads alter dimensions; wall composition changes to unknown material; numbering becomes unreliable
  • Beyond Floor 52: Documentation sparse; see expedition logs
  • Lighting: Unchanged throughout stairwell (60Hz fluorescent, uniform intensity)
  • Temperature: Constant 68°F regardless of reported floor elevation
  • Air Quality: No degradation detected; oxygen levels remain normal

Floor Numbering Anomalies

CRITICAL OBSERVATION: Floor numbering system does not correlate with distance traveled. Multiple researchers ascending from Floor 12 to Floor 13 reported traversing different numbers of stair steps (ranging from 18 steps to 47 steps) despite identical starting/ending points. The stairwell appears to expand or contract relative to directional intent.

Additional anomalies:

  • Floor 7 appears on both descent and ascent routes without backtracking
  • Floor 23 has been documented as appearing twice within single continuous ascent (ascending, descending internally, then ascending again)
  • Descent from Floor 15 occasionally leads to Floor 31 (upward progression during downward movement)
  • Researchers report numbers becoming "sticky" — certain floor markers become difficult to pass, as if the stairwell resists progression
  • Floor numbering beyond 67 becomes illegible in all photographs; markers appear to exist but cannot be read

Field Expedition Log - Entry 092 Alpha

[2005-09-13 / 09:47 - Researcher 156]

Initial solo entry via basement access, Location 092. Descended to Floor 0 marker. Structure presents as standard emergency stairwell. Decided to attempt ascent to establish baseline. Climbed steadily. Counted steps obsessively: approximately 22 steps per floor. Maintained climbing. Reached Floor 10 at timestamp 10:15 (28 minutes elapsed, calculated distance: ~200 meters of vertical ascent).

Something felt deliberate about the stairwell. It wasn't abandoned. It was... waiting. Each floor was identically lit, identically empty, identically silent except for footsteps. My footsteps. But they didn't sound like they were coming from me.

[2005-09-13 / 10:33 - Researcher 156]

Reached Floor 22. Time elapsed: 46 minutes. Vertical distance: ~400 meters estimated. Attempted radio contact with Researcher 71 (surface communication). Reception clear. Reported climbing smoothly. Researcher 71 expressed concern about altitude — surface barometer showed no pressure change despite claimed 400-meter vertical gain underground. Continued climbing.

[2005-09-13 / 11:22 - Researcher 156]

Reached Floor 35. Time elapsed: 95 minutes. Breathing elevated but not panicked. Cardiovascular stress within acceptable parameters. Continued. Reached Floor 47 at timestamp 11:51 (124 minutes elapsed). At Floor 47, architecture changed noticeably. Wall texture became almost smooth. Staircase dimensions appeared to shift — treads seemed narrower. Ceiling appeared lower. Paused to document.

Floor 47 felt like a threshold. I could feel something different beyond it. A change in how the air moved. A change in how the light fell. I almost turned back. Almost. But I had made it this far. And I wanted to know.

[2005-09-13 / 12:04 - Researcher 156]

Continued from Floor 47. Climbed to Floor 52. At Floor 52, wall material became visibly different — smoother, almost translucent in quality, with faint geometric patterns visible beneath surface. Stair treads now visibly altered. Steps appeared steeper. Counted steps: only 16 steps per floor now instead of previous 22. Reported findings to Researcher 71. Long pause before response. Researcher 71 asked me to describe the patterns on the walls. I described them. Silence. Then: "We've seen those patterns before. Don't go further."

[2005-09-13 / 12:31 - Researcher 156]

Attempted to descend. Turned around on Floor 52 landing. Began descending. After approximately 40 steps, I realized the floor marker I was approaching showed Floor 58. I was ascending. I had turned around completely but was still moving upward. Confusion. Verified with Researcher 71 — they confirmed I was still ascending via radio signal triangulation. Turned around again. Same phenomenon. Descent led to Floor 64. Stopped. Sat on stairs for 3 minutes. Breathing exercises. Resumed ascending. No further attempt at descent made.

[2005-09-13 / 13:15 - Researcher 156]

Floor 67. Time elapsed: 206 minutes (3 hours 26 minutes). Floor markers beyond this point became illegible in person — I could see the numbers existed but could not read them. Like visual static. Attempted to photograph markers; all photographs showed blank markers. Reported this to Researcher 71. Asked if they wanted me to stop. Long pause. Researcher 71 said: "Keep going. We need to know how high it goes."

That request broke something in me. Not fear exactly. But a recognition that this wasn't about discovery anymore. This was about documentation. This was about cataloging something that didn't want to be cataloged. And I was being used to do it. But I kept climbing.

[2005-09-13 / 14:47 - Researcher 156]

Floor 73 (estimated — markers now completely illegible). Stair treads have become extremely steep — approximately 45-degree angles now. Previous architectural observations suggest treads should be 30 degrees standard. Breathing difficulty. Researcher 71 monitoring vital signs via biometric sensors. Heart rate elevated but stable. Oxygen levels adequate. Proceeded.

[2005-09-13 / 15:33 - Researcher 156]

Physical sensation changed. Weight distribution became asymmetrical — felt like gravitational vector was shifting. Reported to Researcher 71. Response: "Continue climbing. Your position indicates you're at approximately 1.2 kilometers vertical. Standard stairwell should terminate at 10 meters maximum." Acknowledged. Continued.

[2005-09-13 / 16:15 - Researcher 156, Researcher 71]

Radio contact degraded. Intermittent signal. Last clear transmission from Researcher 156: "Walls are different now. They're not walls anymore. They're... membrane? I can see through them. I can see other staircases. Multiple staircases. All different. All going in different directions. Some are spiraling. Some are straight. Some are going backwards." Signal lost.

[2005-09-13 / 16:18 - Researcher 71, Surface Team]

Contact lost with Researcher 156. Biometric signal continued for approximately 12 seconds after audio contact failure. Then all signals ceased. Emergency response initiated. Rescue team descended into stairwell. Reached Floor 47 without incident (standard climb time: 45 minutes). Beyond Floor 47, rescue team reported architectural changes consistent with Researcher 156's descriptions. Continued upward. Reached Floor 52 at estimated time 17:04. Rescue team then reported same phenomenon as Researcher 156: descent attempts resulted in upward progression. Team attempted forced descent. Team supervisor made decision to reverse and exit structure.

Secondary Expedition - Entry 092 Beta (Partial)

[2005-09-14 / 09:00 - Researcher 103, Researcher 71 (Surface), Recovery Team]

Second attempt to recover Researcher 156. Three-person team descended prepared with emergency equipment and mapped descent protocols. Team briefed on previous findings. Acknowledged risks. Proceeded. Reached Floor 10 without incident (25 minutes). Continued. Radio quality remained clear and consistent. At Floor 34, team reported audio phenomenon: hearing additional footsteps not matching team members present. Counted three unique footstep patterns: one from each team member, and one additional unaccounted footstep. Team attempted to isolate audio source. Unable to determine origin. Continued.

[2005-09-14 / 10:47 - Researcher 103]

Reached Floor 47. At this floor, team split decision. Researcher 103 and Researcher 71 (via voice) recommended continued ascent to Floor 52 to gather additional data before retreat. Recovery team supervisor recommended immediate descent. Compromise reached: Researcher 103 would continue solo to Floor 52 while other team members remained at Floor 47 as observation point. Researcher 103 continued alone.

[2005-09-14 / 11:15 - Researcher 103]

Reached Floor 52. Documented wall texture changes. Took photographs. Successfully completed observation phase. Attempted return to Floor 47 team. Descended. After approximately 15 steps, noticed floor marker approaching showed Floor 58. Paused. Reoriented. Continued descent at controlled pace. Documented five additional "false" floor markers while descending: markers indicating Floors 61, 59, 62, 58 (repeated), and 60. After traversing approximately 80 steps downward, re-emerged at Floor 47 landing. Recovery team was present. Confirmed Researcher 103's arrival. Immediate evacuation initiated.

[2005-09-14 / 12:03 - All Personnel, Exit]

Recovery team evacuated from stairwell structure. All three team members returned to surface. Researcher 156 remains unlocated. Status: missing, presumed still ascending.

Recovered Audio Documentation

Fragment of final transmission from Researcher 156, timestamp 15:33:

"[Static] ...other staircases. I can see them through the walls. They're all going up. All of them. But they're not connected. They're parallel. Side by side but... but not the same. Some have people on them. I can see people climbing. They're climbing too. They're not stopping. They never stop climbing. [Pause] I recognize one of them. That's... that's me. But it's not me. It's older. It's... how can that be..."

[Static surge. Audio becomes partially incoherent.]

"...different. Everything is different now. I can see through the floor. Down is up and up is always farther. I'm going to keep going. I have to keep going. I want to keep going. When did that change? When did I stop wanting to stop? [Pause] I think I can see the end now. There's light. There's so much light. I think..."

[Signal loss.]

Personal Addendum - Researcher 71 (Communication Specialist)

I was listening to Researcher 156 the entire time. I heard everything. Every floor, every breath, every moment of doubt. And I kept telling her to go higher. I kept saying "we need to know." I needed to know. When she reached Floor 52 and wanted to stop, I told her to continue. I knew something was wrong. The biometric data was showing impossible numbers. Her heart rate shouldn't have been so stable. Her oxygen shouldn't have been so adequate. Her body was changing in real-time and I could see it in the data. And I told her to keep going. I can't stop replaying her voice from that transmission. When she said "I think I can see the end now" — was she seeing the end of the stairwell or the end of herself? When she said she recognized herself on another staircase, older, still climbing — was that a hallucination or a prophecy? The message in March came from the structure itself. I'm certain of it now. Researcher 156 is still in there. Still climbing. The stairwell isn't a place she's trapped in. It's a place she's merged with. Every floor she climbs, she becomes more part of it. I wonder if all those footsteps we heard on Floor 34 — the extra one we couldn't identify — were future versions of Researcher 156, climbing floors she hasn't reached yet. Or maybe they were versions she's already passed through, echoing downward. I should have told her to stop at Floor 47. I should have pulled her out when I had the chance. But I wanted to know. And now I know that wanting to know is how the archive breaks people.
